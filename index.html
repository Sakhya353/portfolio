    Fling-Step Investigation

    Concept

    Near-fault earthquake ground motions can contain distinctive long-period pulses. Fling-step effects are associated with permanent tectonic offset across a rupturing fault — an idealized fling-step record can show a one-sided velocity pulse alongside non-zero permanent displacement. Interpreting that permanent displacement is sensitive to baseline errors, low-frequency processing, and integration errors, which is why careful preprocessing is central to this work.

    • Understand the physical origin and engineering significance of fling-step motion.
    • Obtain and organize PEER acceleration records.
    • Examine H1, H2 and Vertical components from 101 RSN stations.
    • Derive velocity and displacement histories from acceleration.
    • Identify one-sided long-period velocity-pulse characteristics.
    • Distinguish permanent/static displacement from transient shaking.
    • Develop a reproducible data-processing workflow.

    Future Work

    The extracted pulse parameters could be used to classify records quantitatively, compare fling-step against forward-directivity motions, investigate pulse-period and amplitude relationships, and evaluate how fling-step motions influence structural response — with automated signal processing and statistical classification as further extensions.

    Processing Matters

    Baseline errors and low-frequency processing can influence apparent permanent displacement, and numerical integration can amplify low-frequency error. Candidate fling-step characteristics therefore require appropriate preprocessing and validation — this demo is illustrative only and is not a substitute for that process.
